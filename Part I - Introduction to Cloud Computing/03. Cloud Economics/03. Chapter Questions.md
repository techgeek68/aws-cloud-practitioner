## 3.6 Chapter Questions

**Q1.** A company replaces its on-premises servers with EC2 instances billed by the second. Which change to its cost structure does this represent?

- A. Operating expenditure is converted to capital expenditure
- B. Capital expenditure is converted to operating expenditure
- C. Both capital and operating expenditure are eliminated
- D. Capital expenditure increases while operating expenditure falls

**Answer: B.** *Target exam: AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner.* Hardware purchased as a depreciating asset is replaced by consumption-based charges recognized as they are incurred.

**Q2.** Which cost is most often omitted from an on-premises estimate when it is compared against a cloud estimate?

- A. The purchase price of the servers
- B. The monthly internet connection
- C. IT labor and facilities costs such as power, cooling, and floor space
- D. The cost of the operating system license

**Answer: C.** *Target exam: AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner.* Server purchase price is always counted, whereas staff time, power, cooling, and space are indirect and routinely left out, which is exactly the gap TCO analysis exists to close.

**Q3.** A finance director asks why a cloud provider can offer a lower unit price than the company achieves in its own data center. Which explanation is correct?

- A. Cloud providers operate at a loss to gain market share
- B. Demand from very many customers is aggregated, which raises utilization and purchasing power, and those economies of scale are reflected in pay-as-you-go prices
- C. Cloud hardware is of lower specification and therefore cheaper
- D. Cloud providers do not pay for power or cooling

**Answer: B.** *Target exam: AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner.* This is the "benefit from massive economies of scale" advantage, driven by aggregated demand and smoothed peaks.

**Q4.** A team migrates 20 servers to equally sized EC2 instances, leaves them running continuously, and makes no commitment purchases. After three months, cloud spend exceeds the previous on-premises run rate. What is the most likely explanation?

- A. EC2 is inherently more expensive than owning hardware
- B. The workload was moved without right-sizing, scheduling, or a pricing commitment, so none of the mechanisms that generate cloud savings were applied
- C. Data transfer charges always exceed compute charges
- D. Three months is too short a period for any cost comparison

**Answer: B.** *Target exam: AWS Certified Solutions Architect - Associate.* A one-for-one lift and shift preserves the original sizing and utilization problem while adding on-demand pricing, so savings only appear once right-sizing, scheduling, and commitments are applied.

**Q5.** Over a three-year comparison, an architect finds that the largest single saving from migrating a workload to AWS comes not from hardware but from reduced administration effort. Which conclusion best supports the business case?

- A. The saving is unreliable and should be excluded
- B. Managed services shift undifferentiated operational work to the provider, so labor reduction is a legitimate and often dominant component of TCO
- C. Labor costs should be modeled only for on-premises environments
- D. The comparison is invalid because staff numbers are outside the scope of TCO

**Answer: B.** *Target exam: AWS Certified Solutions Architect - Associate.* IT labor is one of the standard TCO categories, and moving patching, hardware lifecycle, and backup operations to a managed service is where much of the reduction comes from.
